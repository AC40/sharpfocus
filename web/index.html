02 It never sees your screen

Most dimming tools ask for Screen Recording permission. That's a big ask: it means handing an app the right to read every pixel you look at, forever.

Sharp Focus asks the window server to desaturate what's behind its own overlay — the same private compositing path that gives sidebars their vibrancy. The app never receives a frame. If a future macOS release breaks that path, it falls back to plain dimming, which needs no permission either; the ScreenCaptureKit engine is there, but you have to choose it.

03 Three sliders, and rules that pull them for you

Grayscale, blur, dimming. They stack, they're live, and they're in the menu bar so you never open a settings window to nudge one.

Deep Work, Soft Focus and Monochrome are there when you don't want to think about it. Automation ties them to a clock or to a macOS Focus mode: weekdays 09:00–13:00, or whenever Do Not Disturb comes on. An Always in Focus list keeps chosen apps in color no matter what — most people put their music player there.

04 Scriptable, because you'll want to

A global hotkey (⌃⌥⌘F), a CLI, and a URL scheme. Wire it into Raycast, Alfred, Shortcuts, a tmux keybinding, whatever you already use to start your day. 

// shell
sfctl enabled 1
sfctl grayscale 0.8
sfctl mode frontApp

// Raycast, Alfred, Shortcuts
open "sharpfocus://preset/deep-work"

05 Open source, and small enough to read

A few thousand lines of Swift, no dependencies, no analytics, no account, no update nag. If it does something you don't like, the file that does it is easy to find.

How it works

One borderless, click-through overlay window per display sits just above your app windows. A mask cuts holes over whatever is in focus, and the hole geometry is occlusion-aware — a window floating on top of your editor still gets filtered, instead of punching a hole through it. Window frames come from polling CGWindowList at 15 Hz, so there's no Accessibility permission either.

The fast path uses a private CoreAnimation API. It's been stable for years, but it isn't guaranteed — availability is probed at launch and the app falls back on its own if it ever goes away.
